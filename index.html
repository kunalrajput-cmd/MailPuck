Email is not administrative.
It is cognitive.

Writing a short reply requires far more than typing. You recall past conversations, gauge tone, protect relationships, clarify commitments, and decide what to leave unsaid. Six steps happen before a single word is typed, whether the message takes ten seconds or ten minutes, and that hidden effort accumulates, message after message.

Three separate bodies of research point to the same conclusion: email is quietly taking more from you than the minutes on the clock.

What the research actually shows.

None of this is guesswork. Each point below comes from a specific, citable study.

Your sharpest decisions run out before your day does

A widely cited 2011 study of Israeli parole judges found favourable rulings fell from about 65% early in a session to nearly zero right before a break, then reset after it. Researchers call this decision fatigue: the more choices a mind makes, the more it defaults to the easiest option. The exact mechanism is still debated, but the wider pattern, that judgement quality is not constant across a workload, has held up in studies of forecasters and chess players since. Every email reply is a small decision. Clear them first thing, and it's your sharpest hours that pay for it. Leave them for last, and it's whatever's left of you in the evening that does.

Danziger, Levav & Avnaim-Pesso, PNAS, 2011

It isn't five minutes, it's a full day every week

The McKinsey Global Institute found that people in communication-heavy roles spend around 28% of the working week, roughly 11 hours, reading and answering email. That's more than one entire working day, every single week, spent inside the inbox alone.

McKinsey Global Institute, The Social Economy, 2012

One glance can cost you 23 minutes

Research by Gloria Mark at UC Irvine found it takes an average of 23 minutes and 15 seconds to fully return to a task after an interruption, and email is consistently among the most common sources of that interruption at work. For the kind of original, high-quality thinking creative and technical work depends on, a single notification can cost far more than the moment it took to read it.

Gloria Mark, University of California, Irvine

None of that has to be true.

If the reply is already written, there is no decision left to make, no half hour lost hunting for the right words, and no deep work to interrupt. Just a glance, a small edit if you want one, and a click, all in the time it takes to finish a glass of water.

Security and integrity.

Four commitments, stated plainly rather than badged.

Draft-only architecture

MailPuck writes standard native drafts inside Apple's native mailing app. It has no code, interface, or permission to send email.

Zero autonomous outbound communication

Bring your own key (BYOK)

Draft requests go directly to your personal AI provider account using your own key, a personal access code you create yourself in about two minutes. Your data stays governed by your direct provider agreement, never MailPuck's.

Direct client to API architecture

Local desktop execution

MailPuck runs as a lightweight Mac menu bar app. No intermediary cloud server stores or indexes your mailbox threads.

Transparent product roadmap

Requirements for MailPuck Mac.

Built for speed and privacy. Everything runs on your own Mac, and nothing is sent anywhere except your chosen AI provider.

Apple hardware

  • Supported computers: MacBook Pro, MacBook Air, iMac, Mac mini, Mac Studio, and Mac Pro.
  • Operating system: macOS Sonoma, Ventura, or Sequoia.
  • Desktop native: runs quietly as a menu bar tool with near-zero battery draw.

Client integration

  • Client requirement: uses Apple's native mailing app built into your Mac.
  • All office email apps: full support for Google Workspace, Gmail, Microsoft 365, Outlook, Exchange, and iCloud.
  • Draft-only safety: MailPuck never sends an email. Drafts wait in your drafts folder for review.

BYOK architecture

  • Bring your own key: connect your own Google Gemini API key, a personal access code that lets MailPuck talk to Google's AI on your behalf.
  • Two minutes to set up: Google issues it free, and its free tier keeps draft costs near zero for most people.

Coming to more platforms

  • In development: Windows, iPad, iPhone, and Android companions, plus browser extensions.
  • No date yet: each one arrives once it meets the same standard as the Mac app, not before.
